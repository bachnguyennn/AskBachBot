BENFORD FRAUD DETECTION PROJECT: OVERVIEW
Year: 2026 (May). Domain: fraud detection / forensic accounting / machine learning on financial data. Tools: Python, XGBoost, LightGBM, scikit-learn, Isolation Forest, SHAP, Optuna, SMOTE / ADASYN, SEC EDGAR, XBRL, Jupyter. GitHub: github.com/bachnguyennn/Benford-Law-Fraud-Detection.
This project extracts numeric patterns from raw SEC 10-K annual filings, engineers Benford's Law deviation features, and trains an XGBoost / LightGBM ensemble with SHAP explanations to flag companies at risk of accounting fraud. A companion Jupyter notebook is the main narrative report, covering exploratory analysis, the model, SHAP explanations, and limitations.

BENFORD FRAUD DETECTION PROJECT: WHAT BENFORD'S LAW IS
Benford's Law says that in many naturally occurring datasets, such as financial statement figures, the leading digit is not uniform: it is 1 about 30% of the time and 9 less than 5% of the time, following a logarithmic curve. A clean company's first-digit frequencies align tightly with the expected curve, while companies that manipulate their financials often deviate from it, because invented numbers tend not to follow the natural distribution.
Bach's project turns that idea into machine-learning features, measuring how far each filing's digit distribution deviates from Benford's expected frequencies, and combines them with classic accounting red-flag ratios.

BENFORD FRAUD DETECTION PROJECT: SEC EDGAR DATA ACQUISITION
- A rate-limited SEC EDGAR downloader fetches 10-K filings and builds a manifest with fraud labels, respecting SEC fair-access rules. The required User-Agent contact identity is read from environment variables and never committed to code.
- Fraud labels come from 70+ confirmed fraud companies sourced from SEC AAER (Accounting and Auditing Enforcement Releases), filtered to their fraud periods.
- An optional large-panel benchmark uses the JAR 2020 dataset (Journal of Accounting Research replication data), about 146,000 firm-years from 1991-2014 with AAER labels.
The pipeline runs in reproducible stages (download, parse, Benford features, Beneish ratios, train), and raw filings and processed CSVs are regenerated rather than committed.

BENFORD FRAUD DETECTION PROJECT: FEATURE ENGINEERING
- Dual-mode filing parser: XBRL tags for filings from 2009 onward, with an HTML fallback for pre-XBRL filings (Enron- and WorldCom-era fraud cases).
- Benford's Law features: 23 deviation features per filing, including mean absolute deviation (MAD), chi-squared statistics, per-digit z-scores, and a round-number ratio.
- Beneish M-Score: the classic earnings-manipulation score (Beneish, 1999) and its components, plus supplementary financial ratios.
- Honest missing-data handling: pre-2009 fraud filings have no XBRL tags while modern clean filings do, so Bach added an explicit is_xbrl indicator and per-ratio ratio_missing flags instead of letting median imputation silently blur "missing" with "average".

BENFORD FRAUD DETECTION PROJECT: MODELLING METHODOLOGY
Fraud is rare (about 2% of filings), so the methodology is built for severe class imbalance:
- AUPRC as the primary metric, because ROC-AUC is optimistic on imbalanced data and investigators work at a high-precision operating point.
- Company-grouped train/test splits: all of a company's filings go entirely into train or test, so the model cannot memorize a company's reporting style.
- Temporal walk-forward cross-validation on the JAR 2020 panel (1996-2014), with no look-ahead bias.
- SMOTE / ADASYN oversampling with a graceful fallback, an Isolation Forest unsupervised baseline, and Optuna tuning with saved trial history.

BENFORD FRAUD DETECTION PROJECT: RESULTS AND HONEST SCOPE
Results are reported in the companion notebook as grouped-validation AUPRC and lift over a random baseline, with a precision-recall curve and a SHAP summary plot. SHAP shows the most predictive features include Benford MAD and Beneish M-Score components. Exact figures depend on which filings are sampled and the random split, so Bach documents how to regenerate the data and reproduce the numbers rather than quoting a single fixed score.
Honest scope: Benford features alone are a screening and prioritization signal for investigators, not a courtroom-ready fraud detector. The project's value is rigorous ML methodology on a difficult, imbalanced, real-world dataset.
